# Research: Universal, style-agnostic dance foundations and learning paths

**Prepared for:** Forge Dance  
**Research date:** 2026-02-17  
**Scope:** Beginner-oriented mobile/web curriculum that teaches transferable foundations, then branches into styles. This is a curriculum design brief, not medical advice or a validated universal syllabus.

## Summary

There is strong curricular consensus—not proof of a single optimal sequence—that dance literacy includes the body in motion, space, time/rhythm, dynamics/energy, relationships, and the integrated practices of creating, performing, responding, and connecting. Recognized curricula explicitly add body awareness, safe practice, technical and expressive skill, movement vocabulary, choreography/improvisation, appreciation, and cultural context; these are the best-supported basis for a style-agnostic core ([National Core Arts Standards, NCAS](https://nationalartsstandards.org/content/dance-introduction); [Australian Curriculum: The Arts, F–10](https://v8.australiancurriculum.edu.au/media/7105/the-arts_f-10_all-subjects_all-elements.pdf); [National Dance Society framework](https://nationaldancesociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/dance-collection-downloads.pdf)).

Forge should launch an **18-unit common trunk** of short embodied lessons, allow low-stakes style sampling early, and unlock substantive branches after learners can keep a pulse, transfer weight with control, navigate space safely, recall a short phrase, vary one movement dimension, and reflect on meaning/context. The exact prerequisite graph, lesson length, mastery thresholds, and review cadence below are **expert synthesis**, not directly validated as a complete dance-learning system.

## Findings

1. **Use shared dimensions and artistic processes, not one genre’s technique, as the universal layer.** NCAS frames style/culture-independent learning through Creating, Performing, Responding, and Connecting, while identifying space, time, and energy as basic dance elements. Australian, British Columbia, and National Dance Society curricula expand this to body, space, time, dynamics/energy, and relationships. These are descriptive lenses that can accommodate different movement worldviews; they are not a claim that all cultures conceptualize dance identically. ([NCAS introduction](https://nationalartsstandards.org/content/dance-introduction); [NCAS Dance at a Glance](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/Dance%20at%20a%20Glance.pdf); [B.C. Dance Foundations](https://www.curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/arts-education/11/dance-foundations))

2. **Dance literacy is broader than copying steps.** NCAS expects simultaneous creating, performing, responding, and connecting. The Australian curriculum similarly integrates choreography, performance, and appreciation and calls for cultural/historical understanding, technical and expressive skill, and safe practice. An app that rewards reproduction alone would omit major recognized outcomes. ([NCAS](https://nationalartsstandards.org/content/dance-introduction); [Australian Curriculum](https://v8.australiancurriculum.edu.au/media/7105/the-arts_f-10_all-subjects_all-elements.pdf))

3. **The transferable physical base is capacity-relative, not an aesthetic posture template.** Across styles, learners need usable body awareness/proprioception, balance, weight transfer, coordination, mobility, strength, endurance, and recovery habits. IADMS states that all dancers use some or all physical-fitness elements but that demands vary by task, genre, age, and individual; it recommends conditioning alongside technique rather than assuming class alone supplies every fitness need. Its current resource also cautions that full-range stretching should occur warm and that plyometrics should progress gradually. ([IADMS, Kenny, Redding & Rafferty, *Dance Physical Fitness*, originally 2011, revised 7 June 2025](https://iadms.org/media/rwthabxe/iadms-resource-paper-dance-fitness.pdf))

4. **Do not market generic conditioning as proven injury prevention.** A 2025 systematic review found only eight eligible dance-specific prevention studies; strategies and surveillance methods varied too much to determine efficacy, though conditioning has promising initial evidence. A 2022 systematic review likewise found possible benefit but mostly small, methodologically limited studies. Teach preparation, gradual load, recovery, symptom response, and referral as prudent risk-management habits—not a promise that completing lessons prevents injury. ([Critchley et al., 2025, *Journal of Dance Medicine & Science*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1089313X251314987); [Dang et al., 2022, *International Journal of Sports Medicine*](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-1930-5376))

5. **Observable performance plus reflection is more defensible than points based on exposure.** Official standards express progression as measurable demonstrations: maintain beat, repeat rhythmic patterns, use directions/pathways/levels, coordinate body parts, create/refine movement, interpret intent, and apply criteria. ISTD’s introductory ballroom tests prioritize staying in time before technical accuracy, while later levels add figures and technique—useful evidence for rhythm-first progression inside that branch, not across every dance. ([NDEO, *Standards for Learning and Teaching Dance in the Arts: Ages 5–18*](https://www.ndeo.org/Portals/NDEO/Standards%20Documents/Standards_for_L_T_Dance_in_the_Arts(1).pdf?ver=EzFey2kDcl8Q3bSixDTi-A%3D%3D); [ISTD, *Modern Ballroom Syllabus Outline*, 2021](https://www.istd.org/documents/modern-ballroom-syllabus-outline-september-2021/modern-ballroom-syllabus-september-21.pdf))

6. **Feedback helps motor learning, especially when it is actionable and visual, but app confidence should be conservative.** A 2022 meta-analysis of 15 physical-education studies found a modest overall benefit of feedback (SMD 0.47), with visual, combined visual-verbal, self-model, expert-model, and corrective formats showing benefits in sensitivity analyses. This evidence is adjacent to, not specific to, adult app-based dance. Forge can show a model, invite optional self-video comparison, and give one cue at a time; it should not infer joint safety or diagnose alignment from consumer video. ([Zhou et al., 2022, *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192215361))

7. **Interleave only after initial comprehension, and test retention/transfer rather than same-session fluency.** A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of 54 motor-learning studies found a medium overall retention benefit from random/interleaved practice, but the benefit was nearly negligible in applied settings and negligible/non-significant in young people. Therefore, a few blocked attempts for a new action followed by moderate mixing and later retrieval is more defensible than aggressive randomization from trial one. This application to dance is **expert synthesis**. ([Czyż et al., 2024, *Scientific Reports*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3))

8. **Style branches must teach lineage, values, musical structures, social setting, and conventions—not merely swap step videos.** NCAS includes societal, cultural, and historical context; Australia explicitly asks learners to engage with dance from diverse times, places, purposes, and viewpoints. Official genre syllabi illustrate that conventions diverge sharply: RAD ballet specifies codified exercises and vocabulary; ISTD contemporary includes floor/standing/travelling/jumping work and structured improvisation; ISTD ballroom introduces timing, figures, roles, hold, and partnership. These are branch evidence, not universal templates. ([NCAS anchor standards](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/content/national-core-arts-standards-anchor-standards); [RAD examination specifications](https://www.royalacademyofdance.org/exams/examinations-information-rules-and-regulations/); [ISTD Contemporary specification](https://www.istd.org/documents/contemporary-specification/istd-s1502-contemporary-vocational-graded-specification-version-1.2.pdf); [ISTD Ballroom](https://www.istd.org/documents/modern-ballroom-syllabus-outline-september-2021/modern-ballroom-syllabus-september-21.pdf))

## What belongs in the common trunk

The labels below distinguish **evidence-supported curricular convergence** from **design synthesis**. “Cross-style” means broadly transferable, not present or valued identically in every dance tradition.

| Ability | Common-trunk interpretation | Evidence status | Keep out of the universal claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body awareness and functional organization | Identify/mobilize body parts and zones; sense shape, tension, breath, base of support, and a comfortable usable range; coordinate axial and locomotor action | Directly represented across NCAS/NDEO, Australian, B.C., and NDS frameworks | One ideal posture, ballet turnout, fixed pelvic placement, culturally specific body ideals |
| Rhythm and musicality | Detect/maintain pulse where present; start/stop; match tempo; reproduce simple durations/accents; phrase movement; also move intentionally without music | Time/rhythm is a recurring curriculum element; NDEO gives progressive outcomes | Eight-counts as universal; 4/4, downbeat hierarchy, or Western notation as default; assumption all dance uses music or steady meter |
| Balance and weight transfer | Shift center of mass/base of support with control; stabilize; recover; change direction; distinguish full/partial/no weight | Curricular support for balance/control/weight and dance-science support for physical capacity | Genre-specific footwork, rise/fall, “groundedness,” pointe, heel leads |
| Coordination and movement vocabulary | Isolate and integrate parts; bilateral/contralateral patterns; locomotor and non-locomotor actions; connect actions into phrases | Direct curricular convergence | Named codified steps presented as universal |
| Mobility, strength, endurance, recovery | Capacity-relative preparation; comfortable range; controlled bend/rise/push/pull/brace; repeated movement at manageable effort; rest and gradual progression | IADMS directly supports fitness components and supplementary conditioning; injury-prevention effect remains uncertain | Extreme flexibility, body-shape goals, fixed reps for all bodies, “no pain no gain” |
| Spatial awareness | Personal/general space, directions, facings, pathways, levels, size, focus; collision avoidance | Strong curriculum convergence | Proscenium “front,” fixed lines/formations, or counter-clockwise floorcraft as universal |
| Dynamics/quality | Explore contrasts in time, weight/force, flow, texture, initiation, continuity; describe perceived effect | Strong curriculum convergence | Treating one analysis system’s labels as the only valid cultural account |
| Learning, memory, and adaptation | Observe, imitate, chunk, recall short phrases, reverse/transpose/vary, self-assess, respond to one cue | Standards support recall/refinement; feedback and contextual-interference evidence is adjacent | Claim that one count/chunk method fits all; same-session success as mastery |
| Improvisation and composition | Respond to prompts; choose, order, repeat, contrast, and refine movement; explain intention | Directly supported by NCAS/Australian curricula | Contemporary concert-dance improvisation conventions as universal |
| Expression, interpretation, and response | Vary focus/dynamics/phrasing; identify intent and context; describe rather than rank unfamiliar work | Direct standards support | Fixed emotion-to-gesture mappings; equating facial display with expression |
| Relationships and partnering readiness | Mirror, follow, lead, synchronize/contrast, maintain spacing; ask/receive consent; stop safely | Relationships are a common element; consent protocol is **expert synthesis/ethical safeguard** | Mandatory touch, gendered roles, lifts, holds, connection techniques |
| Safety and self-management | Space check, footwear/surface awareness, progressive warm-up, effort check, hydration/recovery prompts, stop/modify/seek qualified help for concerning symptoms | Recognized curricula, IADMS, and Safe in Dance International support this broadly | Medical screening/diagnosis by app; universal pain thresholds; guaranteed prevention |
| Cultural/contextual literacy | Name source community/style, setting and purpose; learn from credited culture-bearers; distinguish adaptation from tradition | Directly supported as a learning domain by NCAS/Australian curricula | A flattened “world dance” category or decontextualized movement extraction |

## Proposed prerequisite order

This graph is **expert synthesis based on the cited standards and motor-learning evidence**. The elements interact simultaneously; the numbering is a product sequence, not a claim about how dance universally develops.

1. **Access and safety gate (always active):** readiness check, clear-space scan, camera/privacy choice, pain/effort guidance, adaptations (standing/seated/supported, low-impact), consent/stop signal.
2. **Body + pulse + weight:** body map and bases of support → comfortable joint actions → pulse/start-stop → full weight shift → step/touch and balance recovery.
3. **Space + coordination:** personal/general space → direction/facing/pathway/level → isolate then integrate upper/lower body → locomotor/non-locomotor combinations.
4. **Rhythm + dynamics:** tempo/duration/accent and silence → simple rhythmic patterns → quality contrasts → apply one contrast to a known phrase.
5. **Phrase learning + expression:** observe/chunk → recall → connect/transitions → focus/intention → delayed recall on a different track or facing.
6. **Create/respond/connect:** constrained improvisation → make a short phrase → revise from a criterion → identify context/source and compare without imposing one style’s criteria.
7. **Relationship readiness (optional solo simulation first):** mirror/shadow → synchronize/contrast → lead/follow as roles anyone may take → consent and no-touch/touch choices. Lifts, dips, floor acrobatics, and weight-sharing require branch-specific qualified instruction and are outside MVP.
8. **Branch unlock:** demonstrated core checkpoint. Continue trunk review inside every branch; do not imply the trunk makes a learner technically ready for advanced demands.

**Dependencies, not hard gates:** Safety wraps every unit. Musicality can grow alongside body/weight work. Creativity and context start in lesson 1 rather than waiting until technique is “complete.” Learners may sample branch culture/music and low-risk signature concepts after Unit 6; only higher-load or partner-dependent content is gated.

## MVP curriculum recommendation

### Format

- **18 common-trunk units**, grouped into six “paths,” each **8–12 minutes**; 3–5 short sessions/week. Duration and dosage are **product hypotheses** to test.
- Each unit contains: **20–40 s concept/context → model → 3–5 focused attempts → one variation → one retrieval challenge → effort/symptom check → 15–30 s reflection**.
- Aim for roughly **80–90% embodied activity and 10–20% integrated theory/reflection** per session. This ratio is **expert synthesis**, not an established optimum. Culture and safety should appear in-context, not as skippable trivia.
- Provide audio-only, mirrored/non-mirrored model, captions, tempo control, left/right labels, seated/supported/low-impact alternatives, and the ability to skip camera use.

### Common trunk: 18 bite-sized units

| Path | Unit | Learner-facing goal | Practice activity | Mastery evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ready body | 1. Space & signals | Prepare a safe practice area; use stop/modify options | 360° space scan; choose impact/support; rehearse stop signal | Correctly identifies two hazards/options and stops on cue |
| | 2. Body map | Move one part while noticing the rest | Head/shoulder/rib/pelvis/limb explorations in comfortable range | Produces 4 prompted part actions without forced range |
| | 3. Bases & breath | Change shape over different bases | Wide/narrow, one/two-point supported shapes; breathe continuously | Holds 3 comfortable shapes and exits with control |
| 2. Time & weight | 4. Find pulse | Move on a steady recurring pulse when present | Tap/step/bounce to 3 tempi; intentional stillness | Maintains a chosen pulse for ~16 beats twice |
| | 5. Transfer weight | Commit and recover | Side/forward/back shifts; step-touch; freeze | Shows clear loaded/unloaded side and controlled return |
| | 6. Rhythm patterns | Reproduce durations and accents | Echo 2–4-event clap/step patterns; movement without music | Repeats 3 patterns, then one after a short delay |
| 3. Space & coordination | 7. Direction/facing | Move safely in more than one direction | Forward/back/side/diagonal with reset | Completes called directions without collision/loss of control |
| | 8. Path/level/size | Transform where movement travels | One action along straight/curved paths, high/mid/low as accessible | Applies two spatial changes to the same action |
| | 9. Parts together | Coordinate upper and lower body | Block feet, then arms, combine; same/opposite-side patterns | Combines a 4-event lower pattern with one upper pattern |
| 4. Quality & phrase | 10. Dynamic contrasts | Change how, not only what | Known 4-event phrase: sustained/quick, light/strong, free/bound | Makes two clearly distinguishable versions and names the change |
| | 11. Learn a phrase | Observe, chunk, connect | Watch 2+2 events; practice parts; link 4–8 events | Performs sequence in order without continuous model |
| | 12. Recall & adapt | Retrieve beyond imitation | Recall next session; change facing or tempo within safe range | Two successful recalls on separate occasions, one varied |
| 5. Make & communicate | 13. Prompted improvisation | Make choices inside constraints | 20–30 s prompt using two body parts and two levels/options | Satisfies constraints while making at least one personal choice |
| | 14. Compose A–B | Organize and repeat an idea | Create two 2–4-event motifs; order A–B–A or chosen form | Repeats chosen structure and explains one choice |
| | 15. Focus & intent | Communicate a chosen intention | Alter gaze/focus, timing, energy, or relationship to space | Observer/self-review identifies intended contrast with stated evidence |
| 6. Relate & connect | 16. Observe/respond | Describe movement without default value judgment | Identify body/space/time/dynamic evidence in two works | Gives two observations and one context-sensitive interpretation |
| | 17. Mirror/lead/follow | Attend and adapt to another | Solo avatar mirror, then optional partner no-touch mirroring; role swap | Maintains spacing, follows stop signal, performs both roles |
| | 18. Capstone & pathway | Combine skills and choose branch | Learn/recall 8 events, transform one element, add 2 events, reflect on source/context | Passes multi-domain rubric below on two days; selects goals/access needs |

Approximate beat counts and event counts are design conveniences, not definitions of dance. For learners who do not perceive audio pulse, use visual/haptic timing and allow intentional movement in relation to breath, silence, or other temporal cues.

### Mastery model

Use **competency evidence, not streaks**, for unlocks. XP/streaks may motivate but should not certify safety or technique.

- **Attempt:** learner completes guided practice.
- **Familiar:** succeeds with model/cues on two attempts.
- **Independent:** performs the target without continuous model and can identify the relevant concept.
- **Transfer-ready:** succeeds after ≥24 hours and in one modest novel condition (different track/tempo/facing/order) without pain or loss of control.

A unit is mastered when all critical criteria are met on **two occasions, one delayed**. This threshold is **expert synthesis**. Offer “continue with review” instead of hard failure, and never treat appearance matching as the sole criterion.

**Capstone rubric (0 not yet / 1 with cue / 2 independently):** safe setup/self-modification; pulse or intentional temporal relation; controlled weight transfer/balance recovery; spatial clarity; sequence recall; one dynamic transformation; expressive/compositional choice; context/source reflection. Proposed unlock: no zero on safety and ≥12/16 overall on two days. The numeric cut is a testable MVP choice, not evidence-derived.

### Spaced and interleaved review

- Retrieve a new unit **next session, ~3 days, ~7 days, then ~21 days**; adapt intervals to errors and inactivity. This exact schedule is **expert synthesis**.
- Start a new action with a small blocked set; later mix **one old rhythm + one old spatial task + the new target**. Increase variability only when the learner can identify the task and move safely.
- Weekly “mix tape”: three 60–90 s challenges across paths. Monthly “transfer quest”: unfamiliar appropriate music/silence, changed facing, or user-created ordering.
- Score delayed independent and transfer attempts more heavily than immediate copy performance. Do not overstate interleaving: applied-setting effects are small/uncertain, especially for youth ([Czyż et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3)).

## Branch points and style-family examples

Families are navigation aids, not claims of clean taxonomic boundaries. A style may sit in several families; branch names and content should be co-designed and reviewed by credited practitioners/culture-bearers.

| Branch family | Earliest sensible entry | Shared trunk reused | Branch-specific content that must not leak back as “universal” |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social partner dances (e.g., selected salsa traditions, bachata, swing traditions, ballroom) | After Units 1–9; Unit 17 for partner mode | Pulse, weight transfer, direction, spacing, role attention | Style-specific timing, rhythmic interpretation, basic action, holds/connections, floorcraft, etiquette, histories; roles not gender-locked. ISTD supports timing-first progression for its ballroom system only ([syllabus](https://www.istd.org/documents/modern-ballroom-syllabus-outline-september-2021/modern-ballroom-syllabus-september-21.pdf)). |
| Club/street/funk and hip-hop dance traditions | After Units 1–12 plus branch-specific cultural orientation | Groove/pulse, weight, isolation/integration, dynamics, freestyle constraints | Distinct lineages and social contexts; groove, bounce/rock, cyphering, vocabulary, musical relationships, etiquette vary by form and community. Avoid presenting commercial “street dance” as the whole category. |
| Concert/theatrical: contemporary/modern/jazz | After Units 1–15; later gate floorwork/jumps | Space, dynamics, phrase learning, improvisation, expression | Technique lineage, alignment/parallel/turnout choices, contraction/release, fall/recovery, floorwork, jumps and performance conventions. ISTD’s own contemporary syllabus acknowledges specific Graham/Hawkins/Cohan and Eastern influences, proving it is one situated system ([ISTD specification](https://www.istd.org/documents/contemporary-specification/istd-s1502-contemporary-vocational-graded-specification-version-1.2.pdf)). |
| Ballet traditions | After Units 1–12; qualified-instructor gate for pointe and high-load work | Body organization, balance, coordination, phrase/musical response | Codified positions/vocabulary, turnout and placement, barre/centre structure, épaulement and school-specific technique. RAD is a genre syllabus, not the universal trunk ([RAD specifications hub](https://www.royalacademyofdance.org/exams/examinations-information-rules-and-regulations/)). |
| Percussive/footwork forms (e.g., tap and selected step traditions) | After Units 1–12; footwear/surface check | Pulse, rhythmic echo, weight clarity, coordination | Sound production, footwear/surface, style-specific rhythmic language, lineage, weight and articulation conventions; hearing-access alternatives needed. |
| Traditional/folk/community-specific forms | After Units 1–9 plus a context module created with appropriate community expertise | Spatial relationship, pattern memory, rhythm where relevant, collective attention | Purpose, protocol, music, attire, participation rights, sacred/restricted knowledge, gender/age/community roles, and whether app instruction is appropriate at all. Do not aggregate as a generic “world dance” branch. |

**Recommended product pattern:** after Unit 6, offer 60–90 s **branch postcards** (history/context + one low-risk concept) without claiming competence. After Unit 12, unlock solo introductory paths. After Units 17–18, unlock optional partnered beginner paths. Keep style-specific physical gates local to branches.

## Safety, inclusion, and product guardrails

- Start every session with space/surface/footwear and readiness prompts; end with perceived effort and symptom check. Encourage stopping or modifying for pain, dizziness, unusual shortness of breath, or instability and seeking an appropriate clinician for persistent/concerning symptoms.
- A warm-up should progressively prepare the activity; do not use long passive end-range stretching as the default opening. Load, jumping, turns, floorwork, inversions, weight-sharing, dips, and lifts need progressive branch-specific teaching. ([IADMS fitness paper](https://iadms.org/media/rwthabxe/iadms-resource-paper-dance-fitness.pdf); [Safe in Dance International, “So What is Safe and Healthy Dance Practice?”](https://www.safeindance.com/so-what-is-safe-and-healthy-dance-practice-2/))
- Avoid body-shape/weight scoring, “ideal” range targets, forced turnout, and gendered role assignment. Use function- and intention-based cues and let users choose role, impact, range, and camera participation.
- Make no automated diagnosis, injury-risk score, or definitive alignment correction from video. IADMS’s standards initiative notes the lack of one broadly accepted dance-specific screening approach and calls for fit-for-purpose, valid, reliable protocols ([Liederbach et al., 2012; updated discussion](https://doi.org/10.1177/1089313X241288998)).
- Camera features should be optional, processed with explicit consent and clear retention/deletion controls. Self-assessment must have nonvisual alternatives.
- Partner content requires explicit opt-in, a stop signal, role switching, personal-boundary checks, and solo alternatives. These consent mechanics are ethical **expert synthesis**, not claims from a universal dance syllabus.
- For minors, older adults, disabled dancers, pregnant/postpartum learners, or clinical populations, commission population-specific expert review; do not merely lower tempo.

## Evidence map: direct support vs. synthesis

**Direct/high-trust support**

- Broad content domains: body, space, time, energy/dynamics, relationships; creating/performance/responding/connecting or choreography/performance/appreciation.
- Safe practice, individual capacity, technical plus expressive development, movement vocabulary, cultural/historical context.
- Fitness dimensions and the need to consider genre/task/individual demands.
- Feedback generally benefits motor-skill learning in physical education.
- Interleaved/random practice can benefit retention overall, with important applied/youth limitations.
- Genre syllabi progress genre-specific technique and assess timing, technique, performance, memory, and/or creativity in differing combinations.

**Expert synthesis requiring product validation**

- The exact 18-unit ordering, unit duration, 80–90% movement ratio, branch unlock points, event counts, two-occasion mastery rule, ≥24 h delay, 12/16 capstone cut, and 1/3/7/21-day schedule.
- Treating consent/role-switch practice as a trunk prerequisite for optional partnering.
- The six branch-family taxonomy and “postcards” mechanic.
- A combined blocked-then-moderately-interleaved lesson pattern applied specifically to dance apps.

## Open questions and recommended validation

1. **Audience:** Is MVP for adults, teens, children, or mixed ages? Motor-learning effects, safeguarding, language, load, and assessments cannot safely be identical.
2. **Goal:** Social confidence, arts literacy, fitness, technique preparation, or performance? The trunk weights should change by goal.
3. **Accessibility:** Which seated, Deaf/hard-of-hearing, blind/low-vision, neurodivergent, balance-limited, and mobility-aid pathways are launch requirements? Co-design rather than retrofitting.
4. **Cultural governance:** Which branch communities will approve naming, history, music rights, movement selection, teacher credit, and boundaries around sacred/restricted material?
5. **Assessment validity:** Can users reliably self-score pulse, weight transfer, and recall? Validate rubrics against blinded ratings from diverse qualified educators; measure inter-rater reliability and bias across body types, skin tones, clothing, mobility aids, and camera setups.
6. **Learning efficacy:** Run a pilot comparing exposure-only progression with delayed retrieval + moderate interleaving. Outcomes: 1- and 4-week retention, transfer to an unfamiliar track/context, adherence, confidence, adverse symptoms, and branch readiness—not just lesson completion.
7. **Dose and safety:** What lesson frequency/load produces learning without excessive fatigue in the target population? Collect symptom and exposure data with clear definitions rather than claiming injury prevention.
8. **Theory balance:** Test embedded 20–40 s context against separate readings for recall, respect/attribution, and movement time.
9. **Music assumptions:** How will timing tasks support nonmetric traditions, silence, live/variable timing, and visual/haptic access?
10. **Qualified teaching boundary:** Define which tasks require live instruction (likely pointe, high-impact acrobatics, inversions, lifts/dips, substantial weight sharing, and some culturally governed material).

## Caveats

- No reviewed source validates a single universal, Duolingo-like dance sequence. The proposal triangulates curriculum standards, dance medicine resources, general motor-learning reviews, and genre syllabi.
- Most standards are school-oriented and several arise in Anglophone education systems. Their convergence is useful but not culturally neutral or globally exhaustive.
- “Elements of dance” terminology often reflects Laban-influenced/Western arts-education traditions. Use it as an optional analytic interface with a wide lens, not an ontology imposed on every community.
- Physical evidence disproportionately represents ballet, contemporary/modern, pre-professional, professional, female, and Western institutional dancers. Generalization to recreational beginners and other traditions is uncertain.
- Immediate performance is not the same as learning; app analytics should distinguish completion, delayed retention, and transfer.
- Safety practices reduce avoidable risk but cannot make dance risk-free; injury-prevention efficacy remains understudied.

- **Czyż SH et al. “High contextual interference improves retention in motor learning: systematic review and meta-analysis.” *Scientific Reports*. 2024;14.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3) — Best recent synthesis for interleaving; importantly reports weak applied/youth effects.
- **International Association for Dance Medicine & Science Standard Measures Consensus Initiative update. *Journal of Dance Medicine & Science*. 2024.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1089313X241288998) — Consensus update showing limitations in screening and injury measurement.
- **Imperial Society of Teachers of Dancing. *Modern Ballroom Syllabus Outline*. 2021.** [PDF](https://www.istd.org/documents/modern-ballroom-syllabus-outline-september-2021/modern-ballroom-syllabus-september-21.pdf) — Official genre evidence for timing-first introductory assessment and later partnered/technical progression.
- **Imperial Society of Teachers of Dancing. *Contemporary Dance Vocational Graded Specification*, version 1.2.** [PDF](https://www.istd.org/documents/contemporary-specification/istd-s1502-contemporary-vocational-graded-specification-version-1.2.pdf) — Official genre example combining technique, artistry, phrase recall, and structured improvisation while identifying its specific lineage.
- **Royal Academy of Dance. Examination Specifications hub.** [Official page](https://www.royalacademyofdance.org/exams/examinations-information-rules-and-regulations/) — Official ballet syllabus evidence; used only to delineate branch-specific codification.
- **Safe in Dance International. “So What is Safe and Healthy Dance Practice?”** [Page](https://www.safeindance.com/so-what-is-safe-and-healthy-dance-practice-2/) — Specialist organization’s applied guidance on environment, warm-up/cool-down, alignment relative to style/capacity, nutrition, and hydration; lower evidentiary weight than systematic reviews.

### Dropped or not relied upon

- **Frontiers, “Learner-centered versus teacher-centered dance pedagogy…” (2026)** — Search result is future-dated relative to much indexed literature and too recent to independently verify publication history; omitted from recommendations.
- **Preschool dance/fundamental-motor-skills review hosted through an institutional proxy** — Inaccessible/noncanonical URL and age-specific population; not used.
- **Commercial dance-course, studio, blog, and SEO curriculum pages** — Not authoritative and often present one studio’s genre assumptions as general foundations.
- **ISTD Street Dance teacher syllabus** — Official within ISTD but a narrow examiner/teacher qualification with contested umbrella terminology; not used as authority for hip-hop/street cultural scope.
- **Single small intervention studies and narrative claims that conditioning prevents injury** — Superseded by systematic reviews showing heterogeneity and insufficient efficacy evidence.

## Gaps

There is no globally representative consensus curriculum, no validated app-based prerequisite graph, and little direct evidence on spaced/interleaved practice or automated feedback for dance novices. Evidence is especially thin for disabled dancers, recreational adults, older beginners, many non-Western/community traditions, and culturally grounded evaluation. Next steps should be practitioner/culture-bearer review across proposed branches, accessibility co-design, safety review by a dance-medicine professional, and a prospective MVP study measuring delayed retention, transfer, engagement, and adverse symptoms.
